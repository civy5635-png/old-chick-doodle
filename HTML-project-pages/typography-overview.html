Typography Overview

What Is Typography?

Typography is the art and technique of arranging type so written language is clear, readable, and visually appealing. It includes choosing typefaces, setting font sizes, adjusting spacing, organizing text hierarchy, and making sure words are easy to understand on a page or screen.

Good typography helps guide the viewer through the content. It can make a design feel formal, playful, modern, elegant, bold, or calm. Poor typography can make even good information difficult to read or understand.

Key Typography Terms

Typography
The design and arrangement of text to make written content readable and visually effective.
Typeface
A complete design style for letters, numbers, and symbols, such as Arial, Times New Roman, or Garamond.
Font
A specific version of a typeface, including its size, weight, and style.
Serif
A small decorative stroke or “foot” at the end of a letter stroke.
Sans Serif
A typeface without decorative strokes at the ends of letters.
Baseline
The invisible line that most letters sit on.
X-Height
The height of lowercase letters, usually measured by the lowercase letter x.
Cap Height
The height of uppercase letters from the baseline to the top of the letter.
Ascender
The part of a lowercase letter that rises above the x-height, such as in b, d, or h.
Descender
The part of a lowercase letter that extends below the baseline, such as in g, p, or y.
Kerning
The spacing between individual pairs of letters.
Tracking
The overall spacing between letters across a word, line, or paragraph.
Leading
The vertical space between lines of text.
Hierarchy
The visual organization of text that shows which information is most important.
Alignment
The way text is positioned, such as left-aligned, centered, right-aligned, or justified.
